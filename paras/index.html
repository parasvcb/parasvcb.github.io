Research focus

Research focus

Omics measurements of different molecular layers inform the state of a cell or tissue, whether measured in bulk or at single-cell resolution. Each readout is informative within its intended scope, but its interpretation remains tied to procedure and, importantly, context. Even at their best technical capacity, individual omics describe specific attributes of cell state rather than cell in its entirety. This becomes evident when one molecular layer is used to infer another, where regulatory processes and temporal separation can weaken correspondence independently of technical variation; see for instance, transcriptional and proteomic states (Furtwängler et al., 2025; PMID: 40839704; Barry et al., 2026; PMID: 41317903).

In this respect, I am developing interpretive frameworks with aim to better characterize correspondence across omics layers. I began at the protein level, using computational structural biology and MD simulations to study protein structure, dynamics, and function. The central framework I developed now provides a route to ask how regulatory processes modulate RNA and protein diversity within cells to establish identity and shape adaptation to stimuli. It examines how transcript diversity arising from transcriptional and post transcriptional processes, including alternative transcription and splicing may propagate to - or become uncoupled at proteomic level. At present, it characterizes this diversity; its next extension will determine which transcript-level events are realized as protein isoforms or functional variation. I view this as the first module in a broader chain connecting transcript variation to protein state and, ultimately, cellular behavior.

In parallel, I am extending this work toward systems-level cellular processes, presently using cancer and autoimmunity as systems where altered regulatory and protein states have phenotypic consequences. This gives me two complementary directions: to understand disease-relevant constraints on state transitions and studying whether protein conformational distributions help explain cellular heterogeneity. Broadly, I aim to use these insights to improve multiscale representations of biological state, including how such states vary between individuals, respond to perturbation, and change over time.

ENACT and ENACTdb · 2019–2025

Exon nomenclature and classification of transcripts

ENACT combines exon positions and protein-coding sequences in a gene-wide classification. It records coding status, inclusion across isoforms and splice-site variation, including overlapping changes that are difficult to interpret from exon boundaries alone.

I developed the framework to classify exon attributes across transcripts and distinguish changes in coding regions, splice sites and amino-acid sequences. An exon can alternate between coding and noncoding states or contribute different amino-acid sequences in different transcripts; ENACT keeps these cases explicit.

ENACTdb displays exon variation alongside coding sequences, protein domains, predicted secondary structure and disordered regions, allowing individual transcript differences to be examined in their protein context.

  • Annotated protein-coding genes across C. elegans, D. melanogaster, D. rerio, M. musculus, and H. sapiens.
  • Exon classes retain coding status, amino-acid changes and splice-site shifts alongside domains, disordered regions, motifs and other protein annotations.
  • Published the framework in Genome Research and the database in Bioinformatics Advances.

Molecular dynamics · 2020

Mutation-linked dynamics in Vibrio cholerae cytolysin

We studied how a hinge-region mutation affects oligomerization and pore formation in this multidomain toxin.

Molecular dynamics and structural network analysis compared wild-type VCC with Y321A and tracked mutation-associated changes in residue motions and domain contacts.

The analysis suggested how perturbation of a local hydrophobic pocket could alter domain communication required for pore formation.

Steered molecular dynamics · 2021

Sequence design and mechanical failure in silk beta-sheet nanocrystals

We examined how sequence and packing influence fracture under force in silk beta-sheet nanocrystals.

Natural silk nanocrystals were compared with in silico beta-sheet models inspired by globular proteins, using constant-velocity pulling simulations to follow fracture.

The study examined sequence and packing choices relevant to silk-like biomaterials. Stronger side-chain interactions did not necessarily improve backbone hydrogen-bond cooperativity or mechanical strength.

Methods

Transcriptomics and annotation

Build and compare transcript models from GTF/GFF, RefSeq and Ensembl annotations; identify open reading frames and coding regions; classify exon variation.

Proteoform interpretation

Compare transcript-derived amino-acid sequences for changes in domains, intrinsically disordered regions, motifs and localization-relevant segments, including changes associated with altered splice sites.

Structural biology and MD

Molecular dynamics and steered MD, residue contact analysis, PCA, centrality and community analysis for protein motions, domain interactions and mutation-associated structural changes.

Programming and web resources

I build analysis scripts and web resources for biological data. My tools include Python and R for analysis, and JavaScript, React, Django and MySQL for web development.
